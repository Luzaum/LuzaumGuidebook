Oral Laxatives The mode of action of laxatives is based on 
stimulating fluid accumulation in the colon, thereby increasing 
motility.
Bulk-Forming Laxatives Fiber preparations usually con-
sist of polysaccharides and psyllium that are poorly absorbed 
(ch. 165). ey can either be a dietary ingredient or a supplement 
(e.g., psyllium 1-4 teaspoons [5-20 mL] per meal or pumpkin 1-4 
tablespoons [15-60 mL] per meal). ese are only eective if the 
animal is well-hydrated and eating. In patients where megacolon 
has already developed, ber supplements should be avoided, and 
a low-residue diet should be fed instead to reduce the amount of 
fecal material produced.
Hyperosmotic Laxatives ese include indigestible poly-
saccharides (e.g., lactose, lactulose), magnesium salts (e.g., mag-
nesium citrate, magnesium hydroxide, magnesium sulfate), and 
polyethylene glycols. Lactulose (0.5-2 mL/kg PO q8-12h) is the 
most potent and frequently used laxative in this group.

Lubricating Laxatives ese facilitate defecation and inter-
fere with the absorption of water from the colon. Mineral oil is the 
most commonly used lubricating laxative. As aspiration can easily 
occur during oral administration, these substances should only 
be used in compliant and fully mentally responsive animals.240-242
Prokinetic Agents Prokinetics may help improve colonic 
motility in mild recurrent or first time moderate to severe constipa-
tion. However, in the presence of definitive megacolon, no signifi-
cant clinical improvement can be expected from the administration 
of prokinetics.
ese agents stimulate motility throughout the GI tract and 
have relatively few adverse eects. e most commonly used 
preparations contain serotonergic 5-HT4 agonists. Prucalopride 
(0.01-0.2 mg/kg PO q12h) is currently recommended for use in 
these patients.
Misoprostol is a prostaglandin E1 analog that is used as a gas-
troprotective agent to prevent nonsteroidal anti-inammatory 
drug-induced gastric ulceration. However, faster GI transit time 
and diarrhea have been described as adverse eects. Misoprostol is 
an eective anti-constipation agent in humans, and it may be use-
ful in animals with refractory constipation. Invitro studies in dogs 
and cats also show a benecial eect on intestinal smooth muscle. 
While ranitidine, an H2 receptor antagonist, has shown a prokinetic 
eect in invitro studies, this eect has not been conrmed invivo
and this class of drugs should not be used for constipation.238,243-247
Surgery In cats with megacolon, medical therapy is usually 
unsuccessful, and colectomy appears to be the only reasonable treat-
ment. The prognosis for cats that survive the postoperative period 
after colectomy is good, especially if the ileocecal valve is preserved. 
However, self-limiting diarrhea can be expected for a few weeks after 
surgery.237,248-250
