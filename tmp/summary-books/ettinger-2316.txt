PHYSIOLOGY OF MICTURITION
T
he primary purpose of the lower 
urinary tract is to store urine and to 
facilitate its elimination at an appro-
priate time. e bladder stores urine 99% of 
the time and is in emptying phase only 1% of 
the time. Coordination of storage and emp-
tying requires a complex interaction between 
the somatic and autonomic nervous systems, 
as well as normal function of the organs and 
tissues involved.
All 3 components of the peripheral ner-
vous system are involved in the micturition 
cycle (Table 310.1). In addition, conscious 
voiding involves the lumbar and sacral spinal cord as well as the 
brainstem and cerebral cortex.
During lling and storage, activation of stretch receptors 
in the bladder wall leads to a reex arc that increases urethral 
tone and relaxing the detrusor muscle via sympathetic path-
ways (Figure 310.1). During initiation of micturition, stretch 
receptors send aerent signals along myelinated bers of the 
pelvic nerve to the lumbar spinal cord and cranial to the pon-
tine micturition center in the brain. If the situation is appro-
priate for urination, parasympathetic pathways are activated to 
stimulate bladder smooth muscle contraction and parasympa-
thetic signals are inhibited to relax the urethra (Figure 310.2).

mediated contraction of the striated muscle surrounding the
