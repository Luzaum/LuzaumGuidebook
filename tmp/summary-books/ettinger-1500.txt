MYXOMATOUS MITRAL VALVE 
DISEASE
M
yxomatous mitral valve disease 
(MMVD) is a common adult-onset 
valvular heart disease in dogs. e 
disease is characterized by progressive degen-
eration of the mitral valve, eventually leading to 
mitral valve leakage, mitral regurgitation (MR), 
and later to chronic dilatation of the le atrium 
(LA) and le ventricle (LV). Aected dogs have 
a variable progression and outcome: in some 
dogs, the disease progresses to the point that 
they develop signs of congestive heart failure 
(CHF) and other complications, whereas oth-
ers can compensate for the MR for years and 
thereby remain clinically healthy.
OCCURRENCE
Approximately 10% of the total dog popu-
lation develops cardiac disease,1 of which 
MMVD accounts for more than 75% of the 
cases. is makes the disease one of the most 
common causes for morbidity and mortality 
in dogs.2 e disease occurs in other species, 
including humans, cats, horses, monkeys, 
and pigs,3 but the prevalence of MMVD in 
cats is low. e disease has been reported to 
account for the vast majority of the cases of 
CHF in dogs,4 a proportion even consider-
ably higher in predisposed dog breeds.2 5 6
MMVD is encountered in all dog breeds, but the preva-
lence is highest in small to medium-sized dogs, such as Cavalier 
King Charles Spaniel (CKCS), Dachshunds, Miniature Poodles, 
and Yorkshire Terriers.2 5 7 8 e prevalence of MMVD in dogs 
increases with age and is, at a given age, higher in males.2 4 5 9
e disease has an unusually early onset in the CKCS breed and 
closely related breeds: approximately 50% of CKCS dogs aged 6-7 
years and nearly 100% of CKCS dogs aged >10 years have been 
reported to be aected.5 10
UNDERLYING CAUSES AND MECHANISMS
Valve remodeling in MMVD has been well described, but the 
underlying causes for its development and its progression remain 
poorly understood. A combination of a sire and a dam that both 
have an early onset of MMVD has previously been shown to pro-
duce ospring that have, on average, an early onset of MMVD 
(and CHF), whereas a combination of dogs with late onset will 
give ospring that manifest the disease at an old age or never 
do.11 12 According to the currently leading scientic hypothesis, 
supported by pedigree analyses and reports of familial trans-
mission in both dogs and humans,11-13 MMVD is inherited as a 
polygenic trait. However, the genetic architecture of the disease 
is hitherto inadequately explored. Two regions (on CFA13 and 
14) have been associated with early onset MMVD in the CKCS 
breed,14 but these regions are unlikely to solely explain the excep-
tionally high prevalence of MMVD in this breed. Recently, muta-
tional variants near a gene (NEBL), which is exclusively expressed 
in the heart and encodes a protein that is important for stabiliz-
ing and strengthening muscle bres,15 were identied in dogs of 
the CKCS breed. Two of the variants could also be linked to early 
disease onset in the Dachshund breed.16 Disease associated alleles 
were, furthermore, shown to be associated with reduced NEBL
expression in the papillary muscles of MMVD aected dogs. Con-
sidering the pivotal role of the papillary muscle in maintaining 
mitral valve geometry and integrity (see Pathophysiology, below), 
these results could indicate that papillary muscle weakening 
could be a signicant component of the development of MMVD.
Damage to the lining of the endothelial cells covering the valve 
surface17 might play an important role in the progression of MMVD. 
Endothelial cells are known to communicate extensively with suben-
dothelial cells (e.g., valvular interstitial cells [VICs]), and endothelial-
damage–induced release of vasoactive substances might lead to a 
transformation of subendothelial VICs from a predominantly bro-
blast phenotype into more active myobroblast and smooth muscle 
cell phenotypes.18 19 Transformation of VICs has also been suggested 
to be initiated by serotonin (5-HT),19 20 and altered 5HT signal-
ing might be causatively linked to MMVD: experimental animals 
exposed to exogenous serotonin administration are at risk of devel-
oping similar valve lesions as those seen in MMVD-aected valves. 
Furthermore, serum concentrations of 5-HT have been shown to be 
higher in dogs of the CKCS breed,20 21 and higher concentrations 
have been shown in mitral valve and myocardial tissue from dogs 
with MMVD relative to healthy dogs.22 Expression of 5-HT receptors 
and extracellular matrix signaling molecules has also been shown to 
be altered in diseased mitral leaets.19 23
